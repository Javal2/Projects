2026 Global Metal Recycling Market Outlook & Pricing Trends

By Dr. Rajesh V. Sharma, Chief Metallurgist  •  Sept 12, 2026  •  6 min read

Executive Key Takeaways
  • Secondary Aluminium demand projected to grow by 8.4% YoY driven by electric vehicle battery housing and die-cast structural frames.
  • Strict ISRI specifications compliance is mandatory for cross-border shipments under revised EU waste shipment regulations.
  • Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) steelmakers are increasing Heavy Melting Steel (HMS 1&2) scrap ratio to 75% to meet Scope 1 carbon emission caps.

1. Shift Towards Secondary Aluminium Alloys

The global automotive manufacturing landscape is undergoing a structural transition toward lightweighting. Secondary aluminium alloys like ADC12, LM6, and A356 offer up to 95% energy savings compared to primary smelting from bauxite ore. As foundries increase their intake of post-industrial and end-of-life scrap, chemical purity control becomes paramount.

"Recycling one metric ton of aluminium saves approximately 14,000 kWh of energy and eliminates over 8 Metric Tons of greenhouse gas emissions."

2. Optical Emission Spectroscopy (OES) Integration

To satisfy demanding OEM specifications, scrap processors must verify chemical tolerances prior to furnace charge. High-resolution OES spectrometers measure elemental composition down to parts-per-million (ppm), ensuring iron, zinc, and magnesium contents remain within strict threshold limits.

CMR Green Technologies Ltd. is the largest metal recycling company in India.

Scrap metal recycling involves the recovery and processing of scrap metal from end-of-life products or structures, so that it can be introduced as a raw material in the production of new goods.

Metal recycling works by collecting scrap, sorting it into ferrous and non-ferrous categories, shredding it, melting it in large furnaces, purifying it, and solidifying it into bars or sheets for reuse.

Non-ferrous metals like copper, brass, aluminum, and stainless steel yield higher value per pound, while bulk ferrous metals like heavy melting steel and cast iron are also highly valuable in volume.

Scrap metal can be remelted into new alloys, automotive parts, structural steel beams, consumer appliances, liquid metal for foundries, and sustainable industrial packaging.
