Finchbyte

// Route briefing

Context & focus

The supplied documentation describes Finchbyte as a sales and marketing automation platform. Its 15 sections move from the problem and customer journey through the system, creative marketing, automation, CRM technology, people, pipeline, revenue, learning, results, partnership, and separate marketing and sales funnels. The archive does not include user-research notes or interview findings.

Project goals

Make a complex automation proposition easier to follow; connect marketing activity with the sales journey; explain the platform’s stages in a clear sequence. These goals are inferred from the supplied page structure, not from documented stakeholder or user research.

Design approach

A long-form, scroll-led experience. The hero is split into sequential stages, then the page progressively builds the product story through dedicated sections. Interactive simulator and funnel material help explain the service beyond static feature lists.

Key screens & features

The source includes a staged hero reveal, a live sales-journey simulator, problem and journey sections, system and creative marketing sections, automation and CRM technology, people and pipeline views, revenue and learning sections, partnership content, and marketing and sales funnel sections.

UX decisions

The hero sequence is tied to scroll progress rather than autoplay. The documentation describes a typewriter wordmark, physics-driven keyword pills, and a later reveal of the main message and simulator. Separating the marketing and sales funnels makes the two stages easier to distinguish.

Responsive considerations

The supplied stylesheet does not contain CSS media-query declarations. Responsive behavior should therefore be treated as an outstanding QA item rather than a verified result; the long scroll sequence and simulator need explicit narrow-screen checks.

Tools & technologies in source

HTML, CSS and JavaScript; Bootstrap 5.3.3; GSAP 3.12.5 and ScrollTrigger; Matter.js 0.19.0; Lenis 1.1.18; and a Three.js-based MagicRings visual module are referenced in the supplied files.

Outcome & learning

The archive documents a complete 15-section experience and its supporting motion scripts. No launch data, conversion metrics, or measured business outcomes were supplied, so this case study focuses on the documented interface and interaction system.
